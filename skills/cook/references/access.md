# Cooking with what you have

Great food without special gear or rare ingredients. Sections: Baseline kitchen · Equipment substitutes · Ingredient substitutes by function · Flavor bombs · Budget · Shopping access and regional names · Dietary adaptations.

## Baseline kitchen

Assume: stovetop, oven, chef's knife, cutting board, 10-12 inch skillet, medium pot, sheet pan, mixing bowls, measuring cups and spoons, wooden spoon, spatula, tongs.

If they ask what to buy, the upgrades in order of impact per dollar:
1. Instant-read thermometer (removes the most guesswork).
2. Digital kitchen scale (baking accuracy, salt by weight).
3. Cast-iron skillet (searing, crust, oven-to-table).
4. Enameled Dutch oven or heavy lidded pot (braises, bread, frying).
5. Microplane (zest, garlic, ginger, hard cheese).
6. Wire rack that fits the sheet pan (crisp roasting, cooling fried food).
7. Blender or immersion blender.

## Equipment substitutes

## Flavor bombs

Cheap, shelf-stable, high impact. A small kit of these makes almost anything taste finished.

- **MSG**: ¼-½ tsp per pot; pure umami, a third of salt's sodium.
- **Fish sauce**: 1-2 tsp in stews, dressings, marinades, even tomato sauce; the funk disappears into savoriness.
- **Soy sauce / tamari**: salt plus umami plus color.
- **Miso**: in butter for corn or fish, dressings, braises, caramel, cookies.
- **Anchovies**: melt 1-2 into hot oil at the start of almost any savory dish.
- **Tomato paste**: fry 1-2 tbsp in oil until brick-red before adding liquid.
- **Parmesan and its rinds**: grate on top; simmer the rind in soups and beans.
- **Dried mushrooms**: soak in cold water; use both mushrooms and liquid.
- **Chili crisp / chili oil**: spoon on eggs, noodles, vegetables, ice cream.
- **Gochujang, harissa, chipotles in adobo**: instant deep heat and body.
- **Smoked paprika, cumin seed, coriander seed, fennel seed, curry powder, garam masala**.
- **A few vinegars** (sherry or red wine, rice, cider) and **lemons and limes**.
- **Dijon mustard**: dressings, pan sauces, marinades.
- **Capers, olives, pickled jalapeños, quick-pickled onions**: acid plus salt plus texture.
- **Honey, maple syrup**: balance.
- **Toasted sesame oil, sesame seeds**: finishing aroma and crunch.
- **Bouillon paste**: better than most boxed stock; watch sodium.
- **Coconut milk, peanut butter**: instant body for curries and sauces.
- **Frozen peas, spinach, corn**: vegetables on demand.
- **Garlic, ginger, scallions, cilantro or parsley**: the fresh core.

## Budget

- **Cheap proteins**: eggs, chicken thighs and legs, whole chickens, dried and canned beans, lentils, canned fish (sardines, tuna, salmon, mackerel), tofu, pork shoulder, ground meat stretched with lentils or finely chopped mushrooms (50/50 still tastes meaty).
- **Cheap vegetables**: cabbage, carrots, onions, potatoes, sweet potatoes, frozen vegetables (nutritionally comparable to fresh), seasonal produce, bananas, apples.
- Buy spices in bulk or from international grocers, where they are much cheaper.
- Use the whole thing: chicken carcass and onion skins and herb stems for stock, broccoli stems peeled and sliced, stale bread for crumbs and croutons, parmesan rinds for soup.
- Cook once, eat several times (see `meal-planning.md`). A pot of beans is three meals.
- Flavor comes from technique (browning, reduction, acid, salt to target) more than from expensive ingredients.

## Shopping access and regional names

- Design for an ordinary supermarket. If an ingredient might be hard to find, name a swap inline: "gochujang (Asian aisle; or sriracha + miso)".
- Regional names: US heavy cream = UK double cream; US all-purpose flour = UK plain flour; US cilantro = UK coriander; eggplant = aubergine; zucchini = courgette; scallion = spring onion; arugula = rocket; powdered sugar = icing sugar; superfine sugar ≈ caster sugar; broil = grill (UK); cornstarch = cornflour (UK); shrimp = prawns.
- Outside the US, give metric first and adjust cup and tablespoon sizes (`numbers.md`).

## Dietary adaptations

- **Vegetarian and vegan** depth: miso, soy, dried shiitake, kombu, tomato paste, nutritional yeast, roasted and caramelized vegetables, smoked paprika, toasted nuts. Richness: coconut milk, cashew cream (soaked cashews blended with water), tahini, olive oil, avocado. Protein: tofu (press 15 minutes; toss in cornstarch for crisp edges), tempeh (steam 10 minutes to remove bitterness), seitan, beans, lentils, edamame, TVP. Watch hidden animal products: fish sauce, anchovy in Worcestershire and Caesar dressing, gelatin, lard, chicken stock, honey (for some vegans), parmesan (animal rennet).
- **Gluten-free**: tamari instead of soy sauce; cornstarch or potato starch to thicken; rice noodles, GF pasta (cook 1-2 minutes less and finish in sauce), corn tortillas, rice, potatoes, quinoa. Watch malt vinegar, beer, many spice blends and stock cubes, oats unless certified, couscous and bulgur (wheat).
- **Dairy-free**: olive oil or vegan butter for butter; coconut milk or cashew cream for cream; nutritional yeast for cheesy notes; oat milk in baking.
- **Egg-free**: see substitutes above. Eggless binders for meatballs and burgers: panade (bread + milk), flax, or mashed beans.
- **Nut-free**: seeds (sunflower, pumpkin, sesame if tolerated) for crunch; sunflower butter for nut butters. Coconut is not botanically a nut, and the FDA no longer lists it as a tree nut allergen, but some allergists still advise caution.
- **Low-sodium**: build perceived saltiness with acid, aromatics, umami from tomatoes and mushrooms, toasted spices, and salt applied to the surface at the end (surface salt tastes saltier per gram). Potassium-based salt substitutes are unsafe for people with kidney disease or on some blood-pressure drugs (ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics).
- **Low-FODMAP basics**: garlic-infused oil (fructans don't dissolve in oil), green parts of scallions and leeks, chives, asafoetida in place of onion and garlic. Detailed elimination diets need a dietitian.
- **Halal and kosher**: no pork; replace wine with the swaps above; kosher cooking doesn't mix meat and dairy in a dish.
- **Alcohol-free**: see the wine, mirin and sake swaps above; cooking does not remove all alcohol (`science.md`).
