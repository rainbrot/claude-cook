# Baking

Baking is formula chemistry: ratios matter more than in savory cooking, and fixes after the fact are limited. Sections: Rules · Flour and gluten · Sugar · Fat · Eggs · Leavening · Mixing methods · Yeast doughs · Doneness · Common failures · Adapting baked goods · Formula checks.

## Rules

- Give every ingredient in grams, with volume second. Flour by volume varies by 20% or more.
- Room-temperature butter means about 18-20°C (65-68°F): it dents with a finger but isn't greasy. Cold butter for flaky pastry and biscuits; melted butter for chewy cookies and brownies.
- Room-temperature eggs and dairy emulsify better into batters.
- Preheat the oven fully (20-30 minutes; many ovens beep early). Use the middle rack unless the recipe needs top or bottom heat.
- Use an oven thermometer if results are inconsistent.

## Flour and gluten

| Flour | Protein | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Cake flour | 7-9% | tender cakes |
| Pastry flour | 8-9% | pie, tart, biscuits |
| All-purpose | 10-12% | nearly everything |
| Bread flour | 12-14% | chewy bread, pizza, bagels |
| Whole wheat | 13-14% (bran cuts gluten) | hearty bread; absorbs more water |

Gluten forms when flour proteins meet water and are worked. **Strengtheners**: more water (to a point), kneading or mixing time, salt, higher-protein flour, resting (autolyse). **Tenderizers**: fat, sugar, acid, less mixing, lower-protein flour. Cakes and muffins want little gluten (mix just until combined); bread wants a lot.

## Sugar

Sugar does more than sweeten: it tenderizes (blocks gluten and slows egg setting), keeps things moist (hygroscopic), browns, feeds yeast, stabilizes egg foams, and makes cookies spread.
- More white sugar = crisper, flatter cookies. More brown sugar = chewier, moister, deeper flavor (brown sugar is slightly acidic and reacts with baking soda).
- Reducing sugar by up to 25% works in most cakes, muffins and cookies. Beyond that, texture changes (drier, tougher, paler, less spread); add moisture back with fruit purée or yogurt.
- Liquid sweeteners (honey, maple) add water: use about ¾ the amount and reduce other liquid by 2-3 tbsp per cup; they brown faster, so lower the oven about 15°C (25°F).

## Fat

- **Butter** is about 80-82% fat and 16-18% water; the water makes steam for flakiness and lift. **Shortening** is 100% fat: tender, tall and flavorless. **Oil** gives moist cakes that stay soft cold.
- **Creamed** butter traps air for lift (cakes, some cookies). **Melted** butter gives denser, chewier results (brownies, chewy cookies). **Cold pieces** of butter give layers (pie, biscuits, scones, laminated dough).
- Substituting oil for butter: use about 80% as much oil and add a little liquid.

## Eggs

A large egg is about 50 g out of shell: 33 g white, 17 g yolk. Whites set structure and dry; yolks add fat, emulsifiers, tenderness and richness. Extra yolks make richer, fudgier results; extra whites make lighter, drier, springier ones. Eggs also leaven when beaten (sponge, chiffon, angel food).

## Leavening

- **Baking soda** (sodium bicarbonate) needs acid: buttermilk, yogurt, sour cream, brown sugar, honey, molasses, natural cocoa, lemon, vinegar. It also raises pH, which speeds browning and spreading. About ¼ tsp per 125 g flour when acid is present; excess tastes soapy and metallic.
- **Baking powder** is soda plus acid; double-acting reacts once when wet and again when hot. About 1-1¼ tsp per 125 g flour. Too much makes coarse, bitter cakes that rise and collapse.
- **Natural cocoa** is acidic (pairs with soda); **Dutch-process** is alkalized (pairs with powder). Swapping one for the other changes rise and color.
- Leavening loses power with age: test powder in hot water (fizzes) and soda in vinegar.

## Mixing methods

- **Creaming** (cakes, cookies): beat soft butter and sugar 3-5 minutes until pale and fluffy, add eggs one at a time, then alternate dry and wet, ending with dry. Mix just until combined.
- **Muffin method** (muffins, quick breads, pancakes): whisk wet, whisk dry, combine with a few strokes; lumps are fine. Overmixing makes tunnels and toughness.
- **Rubbing in / biscuit method** (biscuits, scones, pie): cut cold fat into flour until pea-sized pieces remain, add cold liquid, mix minimally, fold and press for layers.
- **Reverse creaming** (tender, fine-crumb cakes): mix fat into dry ingredients first, then liquids.
- **Foaming** (sponge, meringue, chiffon): whip eggs or whites with sugar to the stage needed, then fold in dry ingredients gently.

## Yeast doughs

- Desired dough temperature 24-26°C (75-78°F). Use warmer or cooler water to hit it.
- Knead until the dough is smooth and passes the windowpane test (stretches thin enough to see light), or use time instead: no-knead dough (high hydration, 12-18 hours) develops gluten on its own.
- Bulk ferment until about doubled and airy. Proof shaped dough until a gentle poke springs back slowly and leaves a slight dent (springs back fast: underproofed; doesn't spring back: overproofed).
- Cold fermentation in the fridge for 1-3 days builds much more flavor and makes timing flexible.
- Steam in the first 10-15 minutes gives a better rise and crust: bake in a preheated lidded pot, or put a pan of boiling water on the lower rack.
- Enriched doughs (brioche, challah, cinnamon rolls) with lots of butter, eggs and sugar rise more slowly; give them time.

## Doneness

- **Lean bread**: 93-99°C (200-210°F) inside, deep golden-brown crust, hollow sound underneath. **Enriched bread**: about 88-90°C (190°F).
- **Cakes**: spring back when pressed lightly, edges pull from the pan, a tester comes out with a few moist crumbs; 95-99°C (205-210°F).
- **Cookies**: edges set and golden, centers look slightly underdone; they finish on the hot pan.
- **Brownies**: tester shows moist, fudgy crumbs, not wet batter.
- **Cheesecake**: edges set, center wobbles like jelly; 65-68°C (150-155°F). Cool slowly in the turned-off oven with the door ajar to prevent cracks.
- **Pie**: filling bubbles thickly through the vents (fruit pies need that boil to activate the thickener); custard pies wobble slightly in the center.

## Common failures

| Problem | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Dense, tough cake | overmixed after flour, too little leavening, butter too cold or too warm when creamed |
| Domed and cracked cake | oven too hot, too much leavening, pan too small |
| Sunken cake | underbaked, too much leavening or sugar, door opened early |
| Dry cake | overbaked, flour scooped heavy, too little fat or sugar |
| Flat cookies | butter too warm, too little flour, too much sugar, no chill, greased pan |
| Puffy, cakey cookies | too much flour, too little butter, overcreamed |
| Tough pie crust | overworked, too much water, warm fat |
| Soggy pie bottom | no blind bake, too cool an oven, wet filling; bake on the lowest rack on a preheated sheet |
| Pale bread | underbaked, too cool an oven, no sugar or diastatic activity |
| Gummy bread | underbaked, sliced too hot |
| Biscuits don't rise | warm butter, twisted the cutter, old leavening, overmixed |

## Adapting baked goods

- **Whole wheat**: swap up to 50% of AP flour by weight; add 1-2 tbsp extra liquid per 125 g swapped and let the batter rest 15-20 minutes. White whole wheat is milder.
- **Gluten-free**: use a 1:1 GF blend that contains xanthan gum, or add about ¼ tsp xanthan per 125 g of a gum-free blend for cakes and cookies (½ tsp for breads). Rest batters 20-30 minutes so starches hydrate. Bread is the hardest swap; use a GF-specific recipe.
- **Egg-free**: flax egg for binding in cookies and muffins; aquafaba (3 tbsp per egg) where lift matters; yogurt or applesauce for moisture in quick breads.
- **Dairy-free**: plant milk 1:1 (add 1 tsp vinegar per cup to mimic buttermilk); vegan butter sticks or coconut oil for butter (coconut oil makes crisper cookies).
- **Less sugar**: cut up to 25%; boost perceived sweetness with vanilla, cinnamon, a pinch more salt, ripe fruit, and warm serving temperature.
- **More protein**: replace up to 25% of flour with whey or a protein blend only in recipes designed for it; protein powders dry out and toughen baked goods. Greek yogurt or cottage cheese in batters is gentler.
- **Altitude**: see `science.md`.

## Formula checks

Run these before presenting any baking recipe:
- Leavening per 125 g flour within range (above).
- If baking soda is used, there is enough acid for it.
- Salt about 1.5-2% of flour weight in bread, 0.5-1.5% of flour in cakes and cookies.
- Hydration fits the product (lean bread 65-75% of flour weight; pizza 60-70%; bagels 55-60%; ciabatta and focaccia 75-85%).
- Sugar and fat ratios close to a known-good formula for that category (see `numbers.md`, Kitchen ratios).
- Pan size matches batter volume (fill cake pans one-half to two-thirds full).
- Oven temperature and time match the pan and product; give doneness cues, not just minutes.
