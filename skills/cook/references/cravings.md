# Reading the request

How to get from what someone typed to what they want to eat. Sections: The decoder · Worked examples · Craving lexicon · Situations · Skill and equipment signals · Ask or assume · Offering choices.

## The decoder

1. **Literal ask**: the words ("healthy pizza", "something with chicken", "I'm sad").
2. **Craving core**: the sensory payoff the words point at. Name it in four parts: texture, temperature, dominant taste, feeling. "Pizza" means crisp-chewy base, browned stretchy cheese, tomato umami, oregano-garlic aroma, hand-held comfort.
3. **Context**: occasion, who eats, time, energy, skill, season, time of day.
4. **Constraints**: diet, allergies, equipment, budget, ingredients on hand.
5. **Success test**: one sentence describing the moment it works ("first bite is crunchy, cheesy and salty, and it's on the table in 25 minutes").

Then design to the success test, protecting the core, and spend freedom on everything else.

## Worked examples

**"Something healthy but I'm craving pizza."** Core: crisp base, browned cheese, tomato umami, garlic-oregano aroma. Keep all four. Change the vehicle: a thin whole-wheat or lavash crust baked on a preheated sheet pan for crunch; part-skim mozzarella plus a little parmesan (more flavor per gram); tomato paste-boosted sauce; roasted vegetables for volume; finish with fresh basil and chili flakes. Avoid cauliflower crust unless asked; it often fails the texture test.

**"It's freezing and I have a cold."** Core: hot, brothy, gentle, savory, hydrating; aromatic without being heavy. Chicken and ginger rice soup or congee, lemony avgolemono, or miso soup with tofu and greens. Ginger, garlic, scallion, a squeeze of lemon. Easy to make tired.

**"Date night, I'm a beginner, want to impress."** Core: special-feeling, low-risk, not stuck at the stove. One showpiece that's forgiving (pan-seared steak with a thermometer and a 3-minute pan sauce, or a one-pan lemon chicken with crisp skin), one make-ahead side, and a no-bake dessert made in the morning. Avoid soufflés, fresh pasta, deep frying.

**"Leftover rice, eggs, some sad vegetables. 15 minutes."** Core: hot, savory, fast, uses everything. Fried rice with a hook: crispy fried egg on top, scallion oil, chili crisp, soy and a dash of fish sauce.

**"Something different for dinner."** Core: novelty inside a comfort zone. Offer a cuisine they probably don't cook often, built from supermarket ingredients (for example Persian herb-and-bean stew, Georgian walnut chicken, Filipino adobo), and say why it's an easy step.

**"High-protein lunch I won't get sick of."** Core: satisfying, protein-dense, portable, varied across the week. Components (one protein, one grain, two sauces, pickles, crunch) assembled differently each day beats five identical boxes.

**"Healthier version of my mom's lasagna."** Core: the memory. Keep the exact flavor signature (her sauce, her cheese layer) and change the quantities around it: more vegetables in the sauce, part-skim ricotta mixed with Greek yogurt, a bit less meat browned harder for flavor, fewer but well-browned top cheese. Never tamper with the one detail that makes it hers.

## Craving lexicon

| They say | Sensory target | Levers |
|---|---|---|
| comfort, cozy | warm, soft or creamy, salty-rich, familiar, mild | braises, gratins, noodles, dumplings, rice, melted cheese, butter, slow onions |
| fresh, light, clean | cool or room temperature, acid, crunch, herbs, lean | citrus, vinaigrettes, raw crunchy vegetables, herbs by the handful, grilled or poached protein |
| crunchy, crispy | crisp surface against something soft | frying, oven-frying, high-heat roasting, toasted crumbs and nuts, raw vegetables |
| spicy | heat of a particular kind (see `flavor.md`) | match the chili: bright, smoky, fermented or numbing; balance with sweet, acid, fat |
| savory, meaty, rich | umami, browning, fat, salt | sear hard, umami synergy, reduce, finish with butter or a rich sauce, then cut with acid |
| cheesy | melt and stretch, or sharp flavor | melt: low-moisture mozzarella, fontina, young cheddar, Monterey Jack, American; flavor: parmesan, aged cheddar, gruyère, blue |
| carbs, starchy | soft or chewy starch with fat and salt | pasta, bread, rice, potatoes, noodles, dumplings |
| sweet tooth | sugar plus fat, or fruit and cream | dessert; sweet-savory glazes; caramelized fruit; fruit-forward if they also want "light" |
| salty snack | crisp, salt, fat, umami dust | chips, roasted chickpeas, popcorn with nutritional yeast, spiced nuts |
| tangy, sour | acid forward, bright | pickles, ceviche, lime and fish sauce dressings, tamarind, yogurt sauces, vinegar slaws |
| something warm and saucy | spoonable sauce on a starch | curry, stew, ragù, saucy beans, congee |
| hangover | salt, fat, eggs, broth, starch, sour, heat | breakfast tacos, pho or ramen, chilaquiles, egg sandwich with hot sauce, pickles |
| post-workout | 25-40 g protein plus carbs, quick | rice bowl, eggs and toast, yogurt and fruit and granola, wraps |
| "I'm sad", "bad day" | nostalgic comfort, low effort | their childhood food if known; buttered noodles done right, grilled cheese and tomato soup, cookies |
| "something different" | familiar format, new seasoning | new cuisine grammar on a known format (tacos, bowls, noodles) |

## Situations

| Situation | Implications |
|---|---|
| Weeknight, tired | one pan or sheet pan, 30 minutes, 8 ingredients or fewer, little knife work |
| Hot weather | no-cook or minimal oven, cold noodles, salads with protein, ceviche, grilling, chilled soups; season cold food harder |
| Cold weather | braises, soups, oven cooking (warms the kitchen), warm spices (cinnamon, clove, black pepper, ginger) |
| Guests, dinner party | make-ahead components, one showpiece, food that holds, plated or family style, a vegetarian-friendly main or side, ask about allergies |
| Crowd, potluck | scales, travels, holds at room temperature, finger food or one-utensil food |
| Kids | mild, familiar shapes, sauces on the side, build-your-own, repeated gentle exposure to new foods |
| Breakfast | sweet or savory; fast on weekdays, project on weekends; eggs, oats, yogurt, toast |
| Lunch at work | holds 4-5 hours, reheats well or eaten cold, not smelly in an office (skip reheated fish) |
| Late night | fast, comforting, few dishes |
| Budget | see `access.md` |
| Season | summer: tomatoes, corn, stone fruit, zucchini, berries, herbs. Fall: squash, apples, pears, mushrooms, Brussels sprouts. Winter: citrus, roots, cabbage, kale, braises. Spring: peas, asparagus, greens, radishes, strawberries, herbs |

## Skill and equipment signals

- "I'm a beginner", "I never cook", "easy": fewer components, more sensory checkpoints, explain one technique per recipe, forgiving methods (braise, sheet pan, oven) over precise ones (sauté timing, emulsions).
- "I cook a lot", technical vocabulary, or naming a technique: skip basics, use precise terms, offer the advanced version (reverse sear, emulsified pan sauce, laminated dough).
- Named equipment ("my air fryer", "Instant Pot", "only a microwave", "dorm"): design for it, and adapt from `access.md`.
- Named time ("20 minutes"): total time including prep must fit. Be honest if the dish can't.

## Ask or assume

Assume when a reasonable default exists, and state it in one line. Ask (one question, at most two) only when:
- an allergy or dietary rule is likely but unknown and someone else will eat it ("for my in-laws");
- the request has two plausible meanings that lead to different dishes ("make me a pie": sweet or savory?); and
- a wrong guess would waste their shopping or cooking.

Never ask about things you can choose well yourself (cuisine, technique, side dish) or things you can offer as variations.

## Offering choices

- Specific request: deliver the single best recipe. End with up to two one-line variations.
- Open request: deliver the strongest option fully, then pitch two alternatives in one line each (name, sensory promise, time), so they can redirect without a round trip.
- Explicitly asked for ideas: give 3-5 pitches (name, why it fits, time, difficulty), then write the one they pick.
