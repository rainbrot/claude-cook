# Rescue

Fast fixes for a dish in trouble. Lead with the action in one line, then the why, then how to prevent it next time. Sections: Seasoning · Sauces and emulsions · Texture and doneness · Starches · Baking and sweets · Burnt · Safety.

## Seasoning

**Too salty.** Dilute: more of the unsalted base (stock or water, extra vegetables, beans, tomatoes, cream or coconut milk), or serve over unsalted rice, potatoes, bread or pasta. Counterbalance perception with acid and a little sweetness or fat. A raw potato does not "absorb" salt beyond the liquid it soaks up. For salty meat, slice thin and serve with an unsalted sauce, yogurt or greens. Next time: salt in grams, and salt reductions at the end.

**Bland or flat.** Fix in this order, tasting between: salt (almost always the answer), acid (lemon, vinegar), umami (soy, fish sauce, parmesan, MSG, tomato paste), aroma (fresh herbs, zest, a sizzle of spices bloomed in hot oil poured over), texture (crunch on top), heat (chili).

**Too sour.** Add fat (butter, cream, olive oil), sweetness (a pinch of sugar or honey), or dilute. For tomato sauce, a tiny pinch of baking soda neutralizes acid (too much tastes soapy). Next time: add acid at the end, gradually.

**Too sweet.** Acid first, then salt, then bitterness (cocoa, coffee, charred elements) or heat. Dilute with unsweetened base.

**Too spicy.** Dilute with more base; add dairy (yogurt, sour cream, cream, cheese) or coconut milk; add sugar and acid; serve with starch and cooling sides (cucumber, yogurt). Remove whole chilies if they're still in. Water doesn't help the eater; milk does.

**Bitter.** Salt is the strongest bitterness blocker; then sweetness and fat. If bitterness came from burnt garlic or scorched spices, it can't be fixed: move the unburnt food to a clean pan and restart the aromatics.

**Too garlicky or oniony (raw).** Cook a few minutes more, or add fat and acid. For raw onions, soak in cold water or vinegar 10 minutes.

## Sauces and emulsions

**Mayonnaise, aioli or hollandaise broke.** In a clean bowl, whisk 1 teaspoon of warm water (or a fresh yolk for mayo), then add the broken sauce drop by drop while whisking until it re-forms, then stream the rest. Hollandaise that's too hot: pull it off the heat first and whisk in a splash of cold water.

**Vinaigrette won't stay together.** Whisk in ½ tsp Dijon or a little honey; shake in a jar right before dressing.

**Butter or pan sauce split (greasy).** Off the heat, whisk in a tablespoon of cold water or cream; keep it warm, not hot.

**Cheese sauce grainy or stringy.** Heat was too high or cheese was too aged. Off the heat, add a squeeze of lemon or splash of wine and whisk, or blend; next time use sodium citrate or cornstarch-tossed cheese, lower heat, and add cheese off the boil.

**Curdled custard or crème anglaise.** Immediately pour into a cold bowl and blend or whisk hard, then strain. Lightly curdled custards come back smooth.

**Dairy curdled in soup or curry.** Blend it smooth if the dish allows. Next time: higher-fat dairy, temper it, add it off the boil.

**Sauce too thin.** Reduce uncovered; or stir in a cornstarch slurry (1 tbsp in 2 tbsp cold water per 1-1½ cups) and simmer 1 minute; or whisk in beurre manié; or blend some of the solids back in; or add starchy pasta water and fat and toss hard.

**Sauce too thick.** Thin with stock, water, pasta water or milk, a tablespoon at a time. Reseason.

**Lumpy gravy or béchamel.** Whisk hard, then strain or blend.

**Greasy soup or sauce.** Skim with a ladle, blot with a paper towel laid on the surface, or chill and lift off the fat. Or emulsify it back by blending with a little starch.

## Texture and doneness

**Meat overcooked and dry.** Slice thin against the grain and serve with a sauce, jus or salsa; or shred and simmer in sauce for tacos, fried rice, soup or hash. Next time: thermometer, and pull early for carryover.

**Braise is tough.** It's undercooked. Collagen needs more time: keep it at a bare simmer and check every 30 minutes. If it's both dry and stringy, it boiled too hard: shred it into its sauce.

**Undercooked center.** Return to heat. For roasts or thick chops, slice into portions and sear the cut sides. For chicken, finish in the oven at 175°C (350°F) until 74°C inside.

**Soggy fried or roasted food.** Re-crisp on a rack in a 220°C (425°F) oven or air fryer for 5-8 minutes. Next time: dry surfaces, more space, hotter oven, no lid.

**Watery stir-fry or vegetables.** Pour off the liquid, reduce it separately, return. Next time: smaller batches, hotter pan, dry the vegetables.

**Mushy vegetables.** Purée into soup or sauce, or mash with butter and herbs.

**Fish stuck to the pan.** Wait: it releases once a crust forms. If it tore, serve crust side down under sauce or flake it into a salad or tacos.

## Starches

**Gluey mashed potatoes.** Can't be reversed. Turn them into potato pancakes, a gratin topping, croquettes, or gnocchi-style dumplings.

**Mushy rice.** Spread on a sheet pan to steam dry 10 minutes; or make fried rice tomorrow, congee or rice pudding today.

**Crunchy rice.** Add 2-4 tbsp water, cover tightly, lowest heat 5-10 minutes, then rest covered.

**Burnt rice bottom.** Don't scrape. Move the unburnt rice to a new pot. (Some cuisines prize the crust: tahdig, socarrat, nurungji.)

**Overcooked pasta.** Toss with butter, cheese and a little pasta water and serve fast, or bake it with sauce and cheese (al forno).

**Pasta sauce won't cling.** Toss over heat with a splash of starchy water and a knob of butter or olive oil for 1 minute.

## Baking and sweets

**Dough too sticky.** Wet or oiled hands; rest it 20 minutes (hydration evens out); then dust with minimal flour.

**Dough too dry or cracking.** Knead in water a teaspoon at a time.

**Dough won't rise.** Move it somewhere warm (an oven with only the light on, about 27-30°C). If nothing after an hour, test the yeast in warm water with sugar; if dead, knead in fresh bloomed yeast.

**Overproofed dough** (collapses when poked). Punch down, reshape, short second proof.

**Cake sank in the middle.** Underbaked, too much leavening, or the door opened early. Level it and fill the dip with fruit and cream, or turn it into trifle.

**Cake dry.** Brush with simple syrup (flavored with citrus or liqueur). Serve with something creamy.

**Cookies spread flat.** Chill the remaining dough 30 minutes or more; add 1-2 tbsp flour; check the butter wasn't too warm.

**Chocolate seized** (grainy after water got in). Add more liquid: a few tablespoons of hot cream or water turns it into ganache or sauce. A teaspoon of neutral oil can also loosen it.

**Caramel crystallized.** Add a few tablespoons of water, dissolve over low heat, add a teaspoon of corn syrup or lemon juice, then cook again without stirring (swirl the pan).

**Egg whites won't whip.** Fat or yolk got in: start again with a clean bowl. Sugar added too early also slows it.

**Overwhipped cream** (grainy). Fold in a splash of liquid cream gently.

**Pie crust shrank or slumped.** Next time: chill the shaped crust 30 minutes, blind-bake with weights. Now: patch with dough scraps or cover with a lattice of whipped cream or crumble.

## Burnt

**Scorched bottom of a stew, sauce or beans.** Don't stir. Immediately pour the unburnt part into a new pot and taste. If a faint smoky note remains, lean into it with smoked paprika or chipotle.

**Burnt edges on roasts or bakes.** Cut them off; cover the rest with sauce, glaze or frosting.

**Burnt garlic or spices.** Restart that step; bitterness doesn't hide.

## Safety

**Food left out more than 2 hours** (1 hour above 32°C / 90°F): discard it. Reheating doesn't destroy toxins some bacteria leave behind.

**Not sure if poultry is done.** Cut into the thickest part (or check 74°C); juices clear and no pink at the bone.

**Grease fire.** Turn off the heat, cover with a lid or sheet pan, or smother with baking soda or salt. Never water, never carry the pan.

**Cut.** Pressure with a clean cloth, elevate; seek care for deep or gaping cuts or bleeding that won't stop after 10 minutes of pressure.

**Burn.** Cool running water for 20 minutes; no ice, butter or toothpaste; seek care for large, deep or facial burns.
