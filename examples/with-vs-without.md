## Without Claude Cook

# Crispy Hot-Honey Chicken Thighs

This takes one pan and about 25 minutes. The cornstarch coating gives the crunch, and the glaze gives sweet heat.

## Ingredients

- **Chicken thighs**, about 1 lb (450 g). Boneless is fastest. Skin-on is crunchier.
- **3 tbsp cornstarch** (flour works but won't be as crisp), with salt and pepper
- **Oil** for the pan
- **Glaze:** 2 tbsp honey, 1–2 tbsp gochujang (or sriracha, or chili flakes), 1 tbsp soy sauce, 1 tsp vinegar or lime juice, and garlic if you have it

## Steps

1. **Prep the chicken.** Pat it very dry. If boneless, cut it into large bite-size pieces. Toss with salt, pepper and the cornstarch until coated.
2. **Fry.** Heat a thin layer of oil in a skillet over medium-high heat. Lay the pieces in a single layer and leave them alone for 4–5 minutes, until deep golden. Flip and cook 4–5 minutes more, until cooked through (74°C / 165°F). Skin-on, bone-in thighs need about 8–10 minutes skin-side down on medium heat, then 6–8 minutes on the other side.
3. **Glaze.** While the chicken cooks, stir the glaze ingredients together in a bowl. Turn off the heat, pour the glaze over the chicken and toss for 30 seconds. Doing this off the heat keeps the honey from burning.
4. **Finish.** If you have them, add sesame seeds or sliced green onion, and something crunchy like crushed peanuts or chili crisp.

**Serve with** rice, or bagged slaw with a squeeze of lime if you don't want to cook anything else.

## Tips

- **For the most crunch,** glaze right before eating, or serve the glaze on the side for dipping.
- **For a hands-off version,** bake at 220°C / 425°F on a rack or parchment for 25 minutes, then toss in the glaze. It's less crispy but takes less effort.

## With Claude Cook

## Crispy Gochujang-Honey Chicken

Cornstarch-crusted thigh bites fried in one skillet, then tossed in a sticky gochujang-honey glaze and topped with peanuts and scallions. You get crunch, heat and a little sweetness from one pan in about 25 minutes.

**Serves** 2 · **Active** 20 min · **Total** 25 min · **Equipment** 12-inch nonstick or cast-iron skillet, 1 mixing bowl

I'm assuming dinner for 2, boneless skinless thighs and microwave rice to keep the effort down. To feed 4, double everything and fry the chicken in two batches.

### Ingredients
**For the chicken**
- 450 g (1 lb) boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch (2.5 cm) pieces
- 1 g salt (⅓ tsp Diamond Crystal kosher · ¼ tsp Morton kosher · a scant ¼ tsp table salt)
- 35 g (⅓ cup) cornstarch
- 45 ml (3 tbsp) neutral oil, such as canola or vegetable

**For the glaze**
- 40 g (2 tbsp) gochujang (use 2 tbsp sriracha if you don't have it)
- 42 g (2 tbsp) honey
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) soy sauce
- 15 ml (1 tbsp) rice vinegar (or lime juice)
- 1 garlic clove, grated
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil

**To finish**
- 2 scallions, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp toasted sesame seeds
- 20 g (2 tbsp) roasted peanuts, roughly crushed
- Cooked rice to serve (a microwave pouch is fine)

### Method
1. **Coat the chicken.** Pat the pieces very dry with paper towels. In a bowl, toss them with the salt, then add the cornstarch and toss until every piece is chalky white with no wet spots. Wet patches steam instead of crisping.
2. **Fry.** Heat the oil in the skillet over medium-high until it shimmers and a pinch of cornstarch sizzles right away, about 2 min. Add the chicken in a single layer with a little space between pieces. Leave it alone for 4 min, until the undersides are deep golden and the pieces lift off the pan easily. Flip and cook another 3–4 min, until golden all over and firm. Thighs are done at 165°F (74°C), but they stay juicy up to 175°F (80°C), so leaning toward done is fine. Move the chicken to a plate.
3. **Make the glaze while it fries.** Whisk the gochujang, honey, soy sauce, vinegar, garlic and sesame oil together in a small cup.
4. **Glaze.** Pour all but about 1 tsp of oil out of the skillet and lower the heat to medium-low. Add the glaze. It will bubble right away. Stir for 30–60 sec, until it is glossy and coats a spoon. Don't go much longer, because the honey and gochujang burn quickly.
5. **Toss and serve right away.** Take the pan off the heat, add the chicken and toss for about 15 sec, just until coated. Pile it over rice and top with the scallions, sesame seeds and peanuts. Eat it within about 10 minutes, while the crust is still crisp.

### Why it works
- **Cornstarch and a short shallow-fry give a crisp crust** without a batter or a deep pot. Cornstarch has no gluten, so it fries up thin and glassy.
- **The glaze goes on at the end, off the heat, and only briefly,** so it coats the crust without soaking it. Thickening it in the pan first means it clings instead of pooling.
- **Gochujang does several jobs.** It brings heat, fermented savory depth and some sweetness. Honey adds more sweetness, and the vinegar keeps the glaze from tasting heavy.
- **The peanuts are a second crunch.** They stay crisp even after the chicken coating starts to soften, and the scallions add a fresh bite to balance the rich glaze.

### Make it yours
- **More heat:** add ½–1 tsp gochugaru or red pepper flakes to the glaze. **Milder:** cut the gochujang to 1 tbsp and add 1 tsp ketchup.
- **Gluten-free:** use tamari instead of soy sauce, and check that your gochujang is gluten-free (most contain wheat or barley).
- **Something fresh on the side with no cooking:** slice a cucumber and toss it with a pinch of salt and a splash of rice vinegar while the chicken fries.
